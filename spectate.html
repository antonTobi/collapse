The whole game is played out headlessly the moment you pick a seed, then loaded as a replay. The Replay dropdown loads a saved game instead: those are stored as a seed plus a move list, so they appear instantly and need no weights file, which is the only practical way to watch the depth-3 agents (playing one of those out in the page takes the better part of a minute). Saved replays have no per-move evaluations - pick the agent and seed directly if you want those.

Review game scores every position of the loaded game with a depth-2 expectimax search over the compressed 39-bank network (17 MB rather than 140, and the same strength), and compares its preferred move with the one actually played. It runs in two passes: a cheap one over the whole game to find the candidates, then a careful one over the biggest forty, which is what decides both the order of the list and whether a candidate was really a mistake at all. The position you are looking at is always re-scored carefully. Every legal move is searched to the same depth (no root cliff), because a deep value for one move is not comparable with a shallow one for its rivals. A dashed outline marks the bot's choice when it differs, with the value it thinks was thrown away by not playing it. The five biggest of those are listed as buttons, and keys 1-5 jump straight to them.

Only positions above a minimum loss are listed, so a clean game lists nothing rather than padding the row out with five harmless moves. The listed ones are real: rolled out over 1 604 held-out human positions, a listed position costs 55 ± 23 points on average against 2 for a position picked at random, and the largest ones (a shown loss above 400) cost about 171. The shown number is the bot's own estimate and runs several times high against a greedy playout, so read it as a ranking rather than as a price - and trust the order of the list far more than the gaps between its entries.

Board walls off a row and/or a column with 6s. A 6 can never be collapsed, so a wall of them is exactly a smaller board - which is how the shorter games used for fast experiments are made. The same networks play them with no modification.

Hover a legal move to see its afterstate: the collapse and gravity, before any new tiles refill the board.
